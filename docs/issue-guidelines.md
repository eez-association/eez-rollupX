# Writing issues

Use this guide for every RollupX issue and update, including research and PM work.
Use the owning repo's templates and CONTRIBUTING.md for triage and board rules.

## Minimum content

- **What and why:** the problem, outcome, or research question, and its relevance
  to RollupX. Give the issue a specific title.
- **Context and evidence:** enough to act without this chat. Link relevant issues,
  PRs, specifications, and decisions. For code claims, identify the revision and
  reproduction or source evidence. State important scope limits and unknowns.
- **Done when:** observable completion criteria, or the evidence needed to answer
  a research question. Optional in Backlog; required before Ready.

Use the template's headings; context can sit within them. Do not add empty
sections. An early idea needs only a clear question or outcome and why it matters.
Maintainers refine it during triage; reporters need not invent implementation plans.

Keep one outcome per issue. Use sub-issues when parts need independent tracking,
and link existing work instead of copying it. A coordination parent states the
overall outcome and closes when required sub-issues and outcome verification are
complete. A review's completion does not mean its implementation findings are fixed.

## Updates and handoff

Updates preserve existing scope and acceptance criteria unless they explicitly
propose a change for approval.

Post meaningful findings, decisions, blockers, or changed next steps. State what
remains and link evidence. Before another person or agent takes over, put relevant
decisions, exact revisions, and limitations in the issue or a linked repo document;
do not rely on chat history or machine-local files.

When closing, summarize the outcome and verification, including remaining limits.
Follow the owning repo's CONTRIBUTING.md for research, cancellation, and board
status handling.

## Write for humans

- Lead with the task, finding, decision, or blocker. Use plain language and precise
  technical terms where needed. Include only what helps someone act, decide, or verify.
- Verify claims against available evidence. Distinguish facts, proposals, and
  unanswered questions. Never invent assignees, deadlines, commitments, progress,
  or source links.
- Omit repeated summaries, generic advice, unrelated suggestions, and empty sections.
- Aim for under 200 words per issue and 80 per update. These are defaults, not limits
  on necessary evidence. Link longer research from a short summary.
- Create documents, labels, and processes only when actual work needs them.

Before publishing: can a teammate understand this without the chat and know what
happens next?
