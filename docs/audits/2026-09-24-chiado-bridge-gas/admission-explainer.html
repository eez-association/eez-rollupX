Composer admission · Chiado measurement

Yes, it simulates. Just not the final bundle yet.

Admission executes each candidate on local chain-state snapshots, then uses gas and time reserves to decide which complete cross-chain groups to prepare. The full ordered L1 bundle is simulated later, immediately before dispatch.

Short answer: “No real simulation” is inaccurate. Admission runs an EVM source simulation and composes its cross-chain effects. That source simulation intercepts the proxy call, so its gas is not a prediction of the source transaction’s eventual receipt. The postBatch number at admission is a conservative formula, not the result of executing the final postBatch transaction.

What happens in order

Choose candidates

At a Sync slot, take a finite held-pool snapshot. Check source state, nonce dependencies, and available time. Automatic mode starts with one group after restart until it has a preparation sample.

Execute source on a fork

Run each candidate through the EVM on pinned L1 and L2 state. Capture the cross-chain call, result, and target effects. Accepted groups advance the local state seen by the next group.

Apply admission limits

Estimate postBatch with base and entry gas pins, shape discounts, calldata, and simulated L1 target gas. Also check companion limits, L2 block gas, blob allowance, and preparation time. Keep groups whole.

Build and prove

Construct the actual L2 Sync block and L1 postBatch for the accepted prefix, encode blobs and witnesses, and obtain the proof. These bytes do not exist at the start of admission.

Simulate final bundle

Run the ordered L1 transactions: postBatch first, then inbound companions. Probe the gas limit, reprice, execute the final bytes again, and validate every intended effect before dispatch.

Snapshot versus chain: These EVM runs use local state pinned to a canonical L1 parent and the L2 Sync block being built. They do not broadcast candidate transactions during admission. Actual receipts come only after submission and inclusion.

Why admission and final execution differ

SOURCE SIMULATION

The bridge call is intercepted

Admission needs to learn the cross-chain effect without consuming the real proxy entry. Inbound: 327,959 simulated versus 398,895 executed. The final nested proxy call used 68,436 gas; another 2,500 net gas around it gives the 70,936 gap. Outbound: 327,786 simulated versus 398,452 executed; 68,166 nested call + 2,500 = 70,666.

POSTBATCH RESERVE

A planning formula, not a receipt

The mixed reserve rose from 181,000 to 611,742 after outbound, then to 843,910 after inbound. It used a 370,000 entry pin for each group and a 140,000 discount on the inbound entry. Final ordered execution required only 401,594. The 442,316 difference is real for this batch, but the entry pin is deliberately conservative.

REFUNDS

Required gas can exceed receipt gas

The inbound-only postBatch used 867,782 gas after refunds but needed 1,084,727 before them—161,571 more than its 923,156 reserve. A rule based only on receipts could accept a transaction whose final execution needs more gas than reserved. Final preflight checks the complete bundle and its actual required gas.

ORDER AND STATE

Grouping changes other components

Mixed L2 inbound delivery used 227,650 versus 179,320 alone: 16,718 more execution gas, 12 more intrinsic gas, and 31,600 less refund. Both destination calls used 35,286. The three L1 posts also settled different prior L2 histories (1,460, 110, and 125 blocks), so subtracting standalone post receipts does not isolate a reusable grouping discount.

So why not just keep adding until the final simulation says stop?

Admission does advance accepted groups on slot-scoped state; the next candidate sees its predecessors. It checks measured preparation time, L2 declared gas, L1 transaction and bundle budgets, blobs, and nonce dependencies as it goes. The exact L1 postBatch bytes and proof are only available after the chosen Sync block is built and proved. Running the full final preflight after every tentative group would repeatedly rebuild and prove candidates inside a short posting window. The final preflight remains mandatory and can reject a candidate that passed planning.

In the mixed experiment, both sources were queued, but automatic admission's cold-start selection was one group after each trace-triggered restart. We temporarily set its existing selection limit to two for the combined measurement, then restored auto. That count limit was separate from gas admission.
